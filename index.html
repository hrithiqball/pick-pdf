Questions

Is it safe to unlock a PDF here?

Yes. The PDF is unlocked inside your browser, on your device. The file and its password are never uploaded and never stored. Read why pick·pdf works this way.

Can it unlock a PDF if I've forgotten the password?

No. pick·pdf removes passwords you already know. It doesn't guess or crack them.

My PDF opens, but won't let me print or copy. Can I fix that?

Yes. Choose it above and those print, copy and edit restrictions are removed straight away, with no password needed.

Which PDFs work?

Any PDF protected with standard encryption (RC4 40 or 128-bit, AES-128 or AES-256), up to 200 MB. Either the open password or the owner password will do.

Questions

How secure is the encryption?

pick·pdf uses AES-256, the strongest encryption the PDF standard offers. It opens in Adobe Acrobat, Apple Preview, Chrome, Edge, Firefox and other modern PDF readers. A long password you don't use anywhere else makes the most of it.

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. It's encrypted inside your browser and the file never leaves your device. Read more about privacy.

Can I stop people printing or copying from it?

Tick “Also block printing, copying and editing”. Most PDF readers respect these restrictions, but some don't, so the password is the real protection.

What if I forget the password?

It can't be recovered, by us or anyone else. Keep it somewhere safe, or keep an unlocked copy.

Why pick·pdf exists

Most free PDF tools online work by uploading your file to their servers. pick·pdf was built because I didn't trust that with private documents.

The PDFs people need to unlock are rarely boring: bank statements, payslips, tax forms, tenancy agreements, medical letters, ID scans. Once a file like that is uploaded to someone else's server, you're relying on their promise about what happens next: how long it's kept, who can read it, and where it ends up if they're breached. pick·pdf does the job without ever needing to see the document.

Unlocking and locking never leave your device

When you unlock or lock a PDF, all the work happens inside your browser, using qpdf, a long-standing open-source PDF tool. The PDF and its password are never uploaded, and nothing about them is stored: not on a server, not in your browser. Close the tab and they're gone.

You can check this yourself. Open your browser's developer tools, watch the Network tab and unlock a file: the only requests are for the page's own code.

Sharing: encrypted before it leaves, deleted after

“Share a file” is the one feature that uploads something, because the file has to reach the other person. It's sealed in your browser first, with AES-256 encryption and a key made from the password you choose. Only scrambled bytes are uploaded, and only for a short time. They're deleted:

  • as soon as the recipient downloads the file,
  • after five wrong password attempts, or
  • when the expiry you picked runs out (7 days at most).

The password, the encryption key and even the file's name never reach the server. It holds encrypted data and no key to open it, so nobody running it, the developer included, can see what you sent. The file is decrypted on the fly in the recipient's browser once they type the password.

What the server can see

For shared files: the encrypted file's size, when it was uploaded, and when it expires. That's what's needed to deliver it and delete it on time. For unlocking and locking: nothing, because no request is ever made. Like any website, the hosting provider (Cloudflare) sees ordinary connection details, such as your IP address, when you load the page.

Don't take my word for it

pick·pdf is open source. Read the code on GitHub and see exactly what it does with your files.
